\section{Failed constraint mechanisms in full}
\label{sec:si-negative}

Three constraint mechanisms that are plausible in principle fail in
practice under the evaluated protocol. The main text
(Section~10 there) summarises each as a scope delimiter; the full
evidence follows here.

\subsection{Sparsemax: severe seed sensitivity under the evaluated
configuration}

Sparsemax \citep{martins2016sparsemax} projects the logits onto the simplex,
producing exact zeros for phases the model deems absent. It satisfies closure
to $\le 5\times10^{-8}$ and achieves the sparsity that softmax cannot. But
under the evaluated training configuration it exhibited severe seed
sensitivity and substantially worse MAE than the other constrained heads:
2--4 times worse than renorm or sigmoid/$\Sigma$, with the three seeds
giving 0.018/0.037/0.117 on Fe--Cr--Mo, 0.053/0.067/0.013 on Fe--Cr--Ni,
and 0.019/0.015/0.079 on Fe--Mn--Ni --- a seed standard deviation of the
same order as the mean (Figure~\ref{fig:si-failures}, panel a).

The observed behaviour is consistent with a \emph{sparsity trap}: once the
sparsemax projection drives a phase's logit below the threshold $\tau$,
the gradient at that logit is exactly zero, so a phase that starts out
clipped can never recover, and whether it recovers is decided by
initialisation. We present this as a mechanistic interpretation of the
observed seed sensitivity, not as a demonstrated cause; the experiment
evaluates one implementation and optimisation configuration. Notably, the
head was trained with a regression loss on its projected outputs --- the
configuration most exposed to that zero-gradient region --- so the
dedicated sparsemax loss and $\alpha$-entmax variants remain untested; a
fixed-scale entmax ($\alpha < 2$) or a warm start from softmax would be the
natural remedies. We therefore do not claim that sparsemax is
unsuitable for phase-fraction prediction in general --- only that it
performed poorly and unreliably under the configuration studied here.

\subsection{Sum-to-one penalty: redundant and harmful}

The penalty head of the main text (ablation study) is included here as a
negative result. The sum-to-one constraint is already satisfied by the
targets; adding it as a soft penalty creates a redundant loss term that
competes with the data fit. At $\lambda = 10$ the penalty dominates the Huber
loss entirely, and the model learns to minimise closure violation at the
expense of phase-fraction accuracy.

\subsection{Residue closure: closure without non-negativity, and the
error concentrates}

The residue head predicts $K{-}1$ phase fractions and closes the sum
algebraically: $\hat y_K = 1 - \sum_{k<K} \hat y_k$. This enforces the
sum-to-one equality by construction but does not intrinsically guarantee
non-negativity: if the other predicted fractions sum to more than one, the
residue is negative. In the stored predictions the residue head emits
negative values on 0.0\% (Fe--Cr--V), 0.02\% (Fe--Mn--Ni), 0.40\%
(Fe--Cr--Ni), 0.50\% (Fe--Cr--Mn), and 1.21\% (Fe--Cr--Mo) of cells, with
the most negative cell at $-0.59$. It is therefore closure-valid but not
simplex-valid, and any deployment would need a projection step after all.

Beyond admissibility, the head is also the least accurate constrained
variant: even the best residue head is 1.4--2.5 times worse than the best
constrained head in the same system, and the choice of which phase to drop
matters --- a sweep over all $K$ choices (Figure~\ref{fig:si-failures}, panel
c) shows the best choice is the dominant phase in four of the five ternary systems. The
observed degradation is consistent with the residual phase absorbing the
accumulated prediction error of the other $K{-}1$ channels; we note this
as an interpretation supported by the sweep, not a directly demonstrated
mechanism.

\subsection{Targeted augmentation does not beat uniform augmentation}

Boundary rows carry roughly ten times the interior MAE, so the cheapest
hoped-for fix is more data where it hurts: 200 extra CALPHAD solves
jittered around the train rows closest to an assemblage change versus
200 uniform-simplex draws, retraining the Fe--Cr--Ni renorm head under
the identical protocol on a frozen test set (three seeds, seeded
initialisations, determinism self-check passes bit-identical).
Uniform draws improve overall MAE on all three seeds (mean $-0.0004$)
and boundary-bin MAE (mean $-0.0010$, about 4\% of the 0.0228 boundary
level); targeted augmentation is mixed in sign on both metrics and
loses to uniform draws in five of six seed-metric comparisons. More
data helps modestly;
targeting it by assemblage proximity does not --- jittered boundary
points are redundant with the existing train rows, while uniform draws
fill undersampled simplex regions. An earlier pilot version is void and
superseded (its augmented rows never entered training and its
initialisations were uncontrolled); the valid comparison above controls
both.

\subsection{Presence-gate temperature scaling does not rescue shift failures}

On Fe--Cr--V X2-shift the gated head loses roughly twofold to plain
renorm (0.068--0.102 against 0.037--0.052 on X2-extrapolation) while
per-phase AUPRC stays at or above 0.98 --- the presence ranking is
intact but the gate values fail. Fitting a presence temperature on
validation rows (best $T$ of 0.22--0.36, i.e.\ validation wants harder
gates) \emph{hurts} test MAE on all three seeds (0.105 to 0.126, 0.094
to 0.098, 0.080 to 0.090); the X2-band shows the same pattern at small
scale. The calibration diagnostic explains why there is nothing to
rescale: mean predicted presence matches empirical prevalence within
0.03 on every phase (e.g.\ 0.739 against 0.745 for BCC\_A2, 0.057
against 0.041 for FCC\_A1). The gate is calibrated on average; its
shift failures are per-row, not global, so no global rescaling fixes
them.

\subsection{GP/RBF baseline: smooth prior, kinked target}

Independent Gaussian-process regressors per phase~\citep{rasmussen2006gaussian}
(RBF plus WhiteKernel,
exact GPR on a documented $N{=}2000$ train subset --- full-train exact
GPR is $O(n^3)$) reach test MAE 0.0233 on Fe--Cr--Ni
(0.0245/0.0229/0.0226 across seeds), against 0.0106 for renorm on the
identical rows: a 2.2-fold gap that the subset cannot explain away (2000
rows is generous for a GP, and the gap exceeds any plausible subset
effect). The GP lands with the unconstrained baselines --- 0.0232
plain sigmoid, 0.0256 $k$-NN --- far behind every simplex-respecting
family (renorm 0.0106, forest 0.0180, XGBoost 0.0212), and its raw
outputs violate closure at 0.031 mean absolute sum deviation before
projection. The stationary RBF prior fights the non-stationary target:
phase fractions are smooth within assemblage regions but kinked at their
borders, exactly where the error concentrates. Smoothness without
structure is not enough; the constraint carries the accuracy.

\section{Detection threshold sensitivity in full}
\label{sec:si-threshold}

The presence threshold $\Np{k} > 10^{-3}$ is a modelling decision. Table~S1
recomputes mean $F_1$ at $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-2}$ from the stored
predictions for the best constrained MLP head and the random forest.
Absolute $F_1$ values move --- stricter thresholds make detection harder
--- but the ordering does not on four of the five ternary systems: the
random forest remains the better detector on Fe--Cr--Mn, Fe--Cr--V, and
Fe--Mn--Ni at all three thresholds, and the MLP on Fe--Cr--Ni. On
Fe--Cr--Mo the better detector flips with the threshold, consistent with
that system's overall tie.

\section{Comparison against published experiments in full}
\label{sec:si-anchor}

Every accuracy claim in the main text is emulation fidelity: the surrogate
reproduces the database. The complementary question is how large the
surrogate's contribution is relative to the database's own disagreement
with experiment. We anchor the pipeline on four published
differential-thermal-analysis (DTA) transition temperatures.

Three Fe--C--Cr alloys with tabulated DTA liquidus, peritectic, and
solidus temperatures come from Drozdov\'a et al.\ \citep{drozdovs2017};
the fourth point is the liquidus of an Fe--21.0Cr--16.6Ni (wt\%) alloy
from Yamada et al.\ \citep{yamada1987}, who also located the
peritectic--eutectic transition point of the iron-rich Fe--Cr--Ni
subsection near 15~wt\%~Cr--10~wt\%~Ni (reported for completeness; not
used quantitatively here). A $\delta$-ferrite fraction dataset with
tabulated metallography was identified but excluded: its alloy (UNS
S31803 duplex stainless steel, Fe--22.07Cr--5.68Ni--3.20Mo--0.17N--0.017C
in wt\%) contains
molybdenum, outside every modelled system, so any comparison would
conflate database error with an unmodelled-element error. All extracted
values, provenance, and caveats are archived with the data release.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Compositions are converted from wt\% to mole fractions (all four lie
inside the corresponding design box), and each alloy is swept in
temperature from 600 to 1900\,\textdegree C --- coarsely at 5~K, refined to 1~K
around every detected transition --- twice: once with the full eligible
phase set on the pycalphad side, and once with the three-seed ensemble of
that system's best head (sigmoid/$\Sigma$ for Fe--Cr--C, renorm for
Fe--Cr--Ni; trained without any knowledge of the anchor values).
Transitions are located on heating as the first sustained appearance of
liquid (threshold $10^{-4}$ on the melting branch) and the last
disappearance of solid; for each alloy the published quantity is matched
to the corresponding transition (Table~S2) --- $T_L$ for
alloy A and the Yamada
alloy, first-liquid for alloy C ($T_S$), and first-liquid for alloy B,
where the published peritectic arrest coincides in both calculations
with the onset of melting (the liquid+$\delta$ field begins 24--26~K
higher, and is therefore distinguishable).

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~S2 and Figure~S2 give the
three-way comparison. Against the full-CALPHAD reference the surrogate's
transitions land within 21~K at the $10^{-4}$ threshold (mean 14--17~K
across the two transition types), and the residual is tail shape rather
than curve shift: at a $10^{-3}$ threshold the mean deviation falls to
6.8--7.3~K, and at $10^{-2}$ to 1.0--3.0~K, while the sweep-wide mean
absolute fraction deviation is $6\times10^{-4}$--$1.4\times10^{-3}$.
Against the published DTA values, the full-CALPHAD reference deviates by
$-3$~K (Yamada liquidus), $+13$~K (alloy A), $-2$~K (alloy B) ---
mean 19~K --- with alloy C the outlier at $+59$~K; adding the surrogate's
error moves the mean to 25~K, no worse than 38~K anywhere. At the primary
$10^{-4}$ threshold the two discrepancies are the same order of magnitude
(surrogate--CALPHAD mean 17.75~K against CALPHAD--experiment mean 19~K,
median 8~K), and per alloy the surrogate term is the larger of the two on
three of the four points (A: 14 against 13; B: 19 against 2; Yamada: 17
against 3; only C reverses, 21 against 59). The database mean is driven by
the single $+59$~K outlier on the most Cr-rich alloy. With $n = 4$ neither
term can be declared the limiting one: the anchor bounds the surrogate's
contribution at tens of kelvin at the detection threshold the protocol
actually uses, not single digits. The four points are too few, and the
DTA method too rate-dependent, for a stronger claim; they are reported
to bound the surrogate's contribution to an experiment-facing pipeline,
not to validate the database.

\section{Predicted phase fields in full}
\label{sec:si-fields}

Figure~S3 shows the predicted phase-fraction fields for
Fe--Cr--Ni under the renorm head, alongside the CALPHAD oracle, for all four
target phases. The surrogate reproduces the phase-boundary structure --- the
BCC, FCC, liquid and sigma fields --- with visible accuracy. The error panel
shows that errors concentrate at phase boundaries, where the fraction changes
rapidly, and are small in the interior of single-phase fields.

We quantified this concentration on the Fe--Cr--Ni test set. For each row
we computed the distance, in scaled $(x_2, x_3, T)$ coordinates, to the
nearest test row with a different active-phase set (a KD-tree boundary
proxy; a differing set was found within 64 neighbours for 92.9\% of rows),
and binned the per-row mean absolute error by that distance. This proxy
measures proximity to regions where the active-phase set changes in the
sampled data; it is not the exact thermodynamic distance to a phase
boundary. Rows in the
nearest third to the boundary proxy carry a mean MAE of 0.022, against 0.008
in the middle third and 0.002 in the farthest third --- a boundary-to-interior
ratio of $\sim$10. In this system, surrogate error is strongly
concentrated near regions where the active-phase set changes, while the
interior of phase fields is reproduced to high accuracy.

\section{Per-model closure violations in full}
\label{sec:si-closure}

Table~S3 reports the mean closure violation
$\mathrm{mean}\,|\sum_k \hat y_k - 1|$ on the test set (mean over three
seeds) for every evaluated head and baseline. Structural constraints
achieve closure to float32 precision; post-hoc renormalisation achieves
machine precision; unconstrained outputs violate closure by orders of
magnitude. Closure is the equality constraint only; non-negativity is
discussed in the main text.
